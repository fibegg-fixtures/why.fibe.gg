# Software distribution

Your WordPress plugin, Shopify app, or ERP extension — running for prospective customers in 30 seconds, with no setup on their side.

If you build a plugin for WordPress, an add-on for Shopify or BigCommerce, a module for Drupal or Joomla, an extension for Odoo or SAP, a custom workflow on top of HubSpot or Salesforce, a theme, an integration — your hardest job isn't writing the plugin. It's getting a prospective customer to *try* it.

Every current path leaks users:

- **"Install our plugin in your WordPress"** → loses 80% at the line *"make sure you have WordPress 6.4+, PHP 8.2, MySQL 8 with a database called…"*
- **"Schedule a demo with our sales team"** → loses 80% of the rest
- **"Watch this five-minute video"** → tells them nothing about whether *their* data fits *your* tool

Fibe closes the gap. You hand prospects a URL. They click. Thirty seconds later they're inside a real, isolated, full-stack instance of the host platform — WordPress, WooCommerce, Drupal, Odoo, a CRM, whatever — with **your plugin already installed**, sample data already loaded, and a logged-in admin account ready to click. They take ten minutes, see if it solves their problem, decide.

## Examples that just work

- **A WordPress plugin developer** ships a "Try it" button next to the landing page. Clicks → fresh WP site with the plugin installed and three demo posts already published. Conversion goes up; engineering didn't have to build anything new.
- **A WooCommerce extension** for booking, calendar, inventory, or shipping — prospects land in a pre-stocked shop where the extension is already running. They actually take it for a spin instead of imagining what it could do.
- **A Shopify-style storefront app** — every prospect gets their own playground with realistic products, not a screenshot deck.
- **An ERP extension for a vertical** (real estate, dental, manufacturing, logistics) — an auditor or buyer walks through the entire workflow inside their own ephemeral copy. No *"let me get back to you after IT provisions a sandbox"*.
- **A custom CRM workflow** built on HubSpot, Salesforce, or Pipedrive — give a demo URL, not a deck.
- **A Joomla / Drupal / TYPO3 module** — same model: a working CMS with your module installed, ready to click.
- **An open-source SaaS-alternative you self-host** (Ghost, Plausible, Outline, NocoDB, Cal.com, Mattermost) — instead of telling people *"read the README"*, send them a working URL.
- **A WordPress theme** with all your custom blocks, ACF fields, and demo content already populated.

## What you get

- **A one-click "Try it" URL** for your marketing site, your readme, your sales emails
- **The instance is real** — same plugin, same host platform, same database engine — just isolated and disposable
- **Pre-loaded demo data** — products, posts, customers, tickets, whatever makes the plugin's value obvious in the first thirty seconds
- **Lifecycle controls** — instances expire on a schedule, on idle, or stay until the prospect commits to install
- **Zero customer onboarding friction** — no *"install MySQL"*, no *"configure HTTPS"*, no *"make sure PHP 8.2 is on your server"*
- **And when they're sold**, the same Fibe bundle becomes the one-line install on their hardware

## Why this changes the funnel

A plugin's value is invisible from the outside. Screenshots don't capture it. Videos partially capture it. Only *touching it* captures it. The old conversion math:

> 10,000 visitors → 200 willing to install → 20 who finished installing → 2 who clicked around enough to buy

With a one-click trial instance:

> 10,000 visitors → 1,500 who click "Try it" → 400 who play for five minutes → 50+ who buy

You haven't changed the plugin. You've just let more of the right people see what it actually does.

> The plugin was never the hard part. Getting someone to install it was. Fibe removes that step.
